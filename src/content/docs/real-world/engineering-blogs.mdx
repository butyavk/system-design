Company engineering blogs show what a team actually built, which problems it had and which trade-offs it accepted. Before an interview, they are also the best preview of the questions you are likely to get, because interviewers often draw on problems from their own domain. This page is a short, curated list grouped by theme. Every link was checked in October 2026 and points to the blog's current home.

<Aside type="tip" title="TL;DR">
- Read the blog of **the company you are interviewing with** first, then two or three companies in the same domain.
- Choose posts about **migrations, outages and scaling limits**. They show trade-offs better than launch announcements do.
- For a broad index of hundreds of blogs, use [kilimchoi/engineering-blogs](https://github.com/kilimchoi/engineering-blogs) on GitHub.
- Add one or two **newsletters** to keep up without checking dozens of sites.
</Aside>

## How to use these before an interview

A week or two before a system design interview, spend a few hours on targeted reading rather than browsing.

1. **Map the company's domain.** Read the target company's blog for the last two or three years. List the systems it describes (feeds, payments, search, messaging, geospatial) and the stack it uses. Interview questions often come from these areas.
2. **Read two neighbouring companies.** For a fintech, read Stripe and Monzo. For a marketplace, read Uber, DoorDash and Airbnb. You will see which problems are common to the domain and which are specific to one company.
3. **Prefer posts about change.** Migrations, outages, rewrites and "how we scaled X" posts show the limits of a design and how a team reasons about trade-offs. Launch announcements rarely do.
4. **Summarise each post in five lines:** the problem, the constraint, the options considered, the choice, and the cost. If you cannot fill in the cost, read the post again.
5. **Practise retelling one story.** Take one post and explain it as if it were your own design session, with requirements, an estimate, a diagram and the bottleneck. This is good practice for the [interview framework](/start/interview-framework/).
6. **Prepare questions for the interviewer.** "I read about your move to Vitess. Is sharding by workspace still a constraint for new features?" shows real interest. Keep it brief, and do not claim more expertise than you have.

<Aside type="note" title="In the interview">
Mentioning a company's own write-up can help, but do not depend on it. Interviewers want to see you reason from the requirements they gave. Use a known design as support ("this is close to how Discord routes requests by channel"), then explain why it fits this problem.

Check the date of anything you cite. A post from 2013 may describe a system the company has since replaced. Saying "they've probably moved on from this since" shows good judgement.
</Aside>
