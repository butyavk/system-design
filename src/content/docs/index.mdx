## How to use this site

<Steps>

1. **Read the [introduction](/start/introduction/)** to see what is where and how each page is built.

2. **Pick a [study plan](/start/study-plan/)** that fits the time you have before your interview, or your own pace, and follow it on the [skill map](/start/skill-map/): it shows what you have covered, what you skipped and what comes next.

3. **Learn the building blocks**: fundamentals first, then traffic, data, caching and messaging. Every topic page covers what interviewers probe and ends with what to read first, what the topic leads to and a **Mark as learned** button.

4. **Practise out loud** on the case studies, using the [interview framework](/start/interview-framework/) and a 45-minute timer before you read the solution.

5. **Keep the numbers close**: [latency](/reference/latency-numbers/), [powers of two](/reference/powers-of-two/) and [availability](/reference/availability-numbers/) tables power every estimate.

</Steps>

## What's new compared to the original

- **Structure.** One README of 1,800+ lines is now 13 sections with a sidebar, search and previous/next navigation.
- **Fresh, current text.** Every page is rewritten from scratch. Topics the original barely touched now have their own pages: object storage, event streaming, rate limiting, resilience patterns, observability, API styles and service meshes, consensus and coordination, and LLM-powered systems.
- **Flashcards.** 150 cards with spaced repetition in the browser replace the original's Anki decks; the same cards download as Anki decks in English and Russian.
- **One shape everywhere.** Topic pages open with a short summary and close with what interviewers ask; case studies end with how a team would build the system in 2026.
- **Diagrams as code.** Mermaid diagrams that follow the light or dark theme.
- **A Russian edition** written as natural technical Russian rather than a literal translation.
