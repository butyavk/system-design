The worked case studies in this section cover a handful of classic problems in depth. Interviewers draw from a much wider pool, but most questions reuse the same few dozen building blocks. This page lists the common ones, grouped by theme, with the **one challenge that makes each question interesting**, the pages on this site that give you the building blocks, and one or two primary sources: papers and engineering posts from the teams that built the real thing.

<Aside type="tip" title="TL;DR">
- Every question has one **core difficulty** (fan-out, ordering, hot keys, exactly-once, geography). Find it in the first five minutes and spend most of the interview there.
- Practise with the [interview framework](/start/interview-framework/): requirements, estimates, high-level design, deep dive, scaling.
- Use the references after you have sketched your own answer, not before, and compare trade-offs rather than copying a company's architecture.
</Aside>

## How to use this list

1. Pick a question and set a 45-minute timer.
2. Write requirements and a [back-of-the-envelope estimate](/start/estimation/), then draw the high-level design.
3. Go deep on the key challenge listed in the table. That is where interviewers push.
4. Read the references and note what they did differently, and why.

<Aside type="caution" title="References are not answers">
Company write-ups describe one team's constraints at one point in time. ## What changed since the original list

- **Dropped:** "design a garbage collector" (a language-runtime question, not system design) and Facebook graph search (the product was wound down long ago; the [social graph](/practice/social-graph/) case study covers the core).
- **Merged:** Facebook chat into the general chat app question; Facebook timeline into news feed.
- **Added:** notification system, video streaming, ride-hailing dispatch, proximity search, typeahead, distributed job scheduler, payment system, metrics and logging pipeline, ad click aggregation and LLM serving — all now common in interviews.
- **References replaced:** most of the original links (Quora threads, Slideshare decks, old highscalability.com posts) were dead or low quality; this list prefers papers and posts by the teams that built the systems.

<Aside type="note" title="In the interview">
- Interviewers rarely want the famous company's exact architecture. They want to see you **find the core difficulty** (for example, fan-out for feeds, ordering for chat, exactly-once for payments and ad clicks) and reason about the options.
- Expect a twist halfway through: "now a celebrity with 100 million followers posts", "now the region fails", "now we need it in real time". Leave room in the design for that step.
- Common mistakes: spending half the time on boilerplate (load balancer, cache) and never reaching the hard part; picking a technology by name without saying what property you need from it.
</Aside>
